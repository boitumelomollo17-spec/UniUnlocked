An alternative to university

TVET colleges, explained properly.

Technical and Vocational Education and Training colleges teach practical, job-ready skills — a real, respected route into a trade or career, not a "backup" to university. Here's how they work, what they cost, and where to find one in your province.

The basics

What is TVET?

South Africa has 50 public TVET colleges, spread across roughly 364 campuses in all nine provinces, all registered and accredited by the Department of Higher Education and Training (DHET). They focus on hands-on training for real trades and occupations — engineering, IT, business, hospitality, agriculture, and more — rather than purely academic study.

Path one

NCV — National Certificate (Vocational)

A 3-year, school-equivalent path (NQF Levels 2–4), usually started straight after Grade 9 or Grade 10. It combines a vocational subject stream with fundamentals like Maths/Maths Literacy and English, and is treated as equivalent to matric once completed.

  • Entry: typically Grade 9 minimum
  • Good for: school leavers who want a practical route instead of the matric-to-degree path
Path two

NATED (Report 191) — N1 to N6

A more work-integrated path, often chosen after matric or while already employed. Each "N" level is a college term, usually followed by required practical workplace experience — completing N4–N6 plus the work experience leads to a National Diploma.

  • Entry: varies by N-level and subject, often Grade 9–12 depending on the course
  • Good for: building toward an artisan trade or technical diploma while gaining real workplace hours

General entry requirements

  • Minimum school grade varies by programme — often Grade 9 for entry-level NCV, matric or specific N-levels for others. Always confirm with the specific college and course.
  • A certified copy of your ID (or birth certificate if you don't have one yet).
  • Certified copies of your latest school results.
  • Some courses run a career interest questionnaire or basic entrance assessment before placing you.
  • Most colleges open applications for the following year around July–September — apply early, since popular courses fill up.
